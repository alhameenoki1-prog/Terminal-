If this were a real attack, clicking would have taken you to a fake login page built to steal your password. Because this is a training drill, nothing happened. Here's what should have stopped you:

The tells in this message

  1. Unexpected "documents ready" hook. A vague notice with no named sender or specific file is bait designed to be sent to everyone.
  2. The link didn't match the brand. The button says "Northwind," but the real destination was secure-verify[.]xyz — a stranger's domain dressed up with brand words.
  3. Reassurance used as a disguise. "We'll never ask for your password" is lifted from genuine mail to make a fake feel safe. Comforting words are not verification.
  4. Urgency to click now. Real documents wait. The push to act immediately is pressure, not service.

What to do for real

Don't click. Hover to check the true link, verify with the sender through a channel you already trust, and report the message to your security team so the domain gets blocked for everyone.
