## 1. Executive summary

**Conclusions.** (1) The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (NTA) commenced on **1 January 2026**. The Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 (NTAA) gazette bracket reads **26 June 2025**, though practice treats 1 January 2026 as operative; both are kept [S1, S2a]. Residents are taxed on worldwide income (s.12), and the disjunctive residency test (s.201) can catch a UAE-based Nigerian with a home or family ties in Nigeria. (2) **Gains are assessed in the year of assessment after disposal** (s.26(3)): a June 2026 gain belongs to 2027, v0.1 erred. (3) The Nigeria–UAE treaty is treated as unavailable: no ratification was found (s.120(1)), and the s.120(5) deeming needs an entry into force we found no evidence of. (4) On the text, WHT on deposit interest is creditable for residents (Treatment A); the "final" reading (B) is unsourced, and both branches are carried together (both refuse today, because the investment-income basis period is unsourced). (5) UAE: no personal income tax; Corporate Tax only above AED 1 m business turnover. (6) US: 30 % dividend WHT with no treaty; estate-tax exposure above USD 60,000 of US-situs stock. (7) Portfolio OS prepares but never files (NTAA s.33); paid preparation may be "practice of taxation" (CITN Act).

### 3A. Accounting and audit controls for a personal ledger

**F1 — The audit assertions transfer to personal data, but the evidence hierarchy inverts (E3/E4) [S44].** ISA 315 (Revised 2019) frames risk around assertions — completeness, accuracy, cut-off, occurrence, classification, existence, valuation. For an individual, bank and broker statements are *external* evidence (strong for occurrence, existence and cut-off) whereas every category label, every "this transfer is a loan to my brother", and every cost basis typed from memory is a *user assertion* (weak). The ledger must therefore store, for every figure, which kind of evidence supports it: statement-derived, document-derived (contract note, dividend advice, payslip), user-asserted, or system-inferred. Doc 4's ingestion layer (C1.3 `journal_entry.source_type`) must preserve this provenance field; without it, nothing downstream can be audited.

**F2 — Append-only journal with reversing entries, never edits (E3/E4).** The posting model is **CANONICAL_CONTRACTS C1**: entries balance per commodity exactly (C1-I1), are append-only (C1-I4), and are corrected by a `void` plus `replacement` entry (C1.7), each carrying who, what, when and why and a link to the original. A bank reversal printed on the statement is its own entry and is not a correction. Editing a posted entry in place is how reconstructed histories become unauditable, and it is how the stale-constant problem in the brief recurs in another form. Categories, counterparties and notes are bitemporal `classification` rows that never touch postings (C1 D4, C1-I12).

**F3 — The reconciliation identity is the primary completeness control (E4/E5).** For every account and period: *opening balance per statement + Σ postings = closing balance per statement*. Failing this produces a named "unexplained difference" line, never a silent plug. Secondary controls: (i) statement continuity — closing balance of statement *n* equals opening balance of statement *n+1*, and page/period gaps are flagged; (ii) inter-account transfers must match on both sides, as two entries joined by an in-transit account, with cross-currency economics tested by the C1.5 residual rule; (iii) control totals per period (count, Σ debits, Σ credits, largest item) stored with each import so re-imports can be compared. These are engineering assumptions; the validation plan in §9 tests them on synthetic statements with planted gaps.

**F4 — Foreign-currency treatment follows the contracts; IAS 21 logic governs presentation only (E3 + E2) [S43, S1 s.34(1)(b), s.201].** Quantities are posted per commodity, and every base-currency figure (translation, revaluation, FX cost) is a derived view (C1 D3, C5). IAS 21's spot/closing/historical logic describes those views; nothing is posted as a revaluation. For tax, the currency pool is weighted-average NGN cost per currency (C1.6), and realised currency gains in NGN are the only FX figure the tax engine receives (C1 D9). This matters because NTA s.34(1)(b) lists "any currency other than Nigerian currency" as a chargeable asset, and s.52(5) bars the chattels exemption for "a disposal of currency of any description" (read 2026-11-04). The rate: s.201 defines "official market rate" as the rate "approved by the Central Bank of Nigeria". The open questions are whether that definition governs gains, and which CBN field applies (R06 m1). The reference hierarchy is C5.1. The rule `NG-FX-OFFICIAL-RATE-2026` is **draft**, so foreign-gain computations refuse until a practitioner answers (§4.2).

**F5 — Investment accounting needs a cost-basis method, corporate-action handling and dual-currency lots (E4).** Lots, fees and NGN tax cost follow **C1.6**: FIFO lots with fees capitalised, and NGN tax cost at the acquisition-date official rate. The method is a versioned rule parameter recorded on every run. Nigerian law does not, in the text we read, prescribe FIFO or weighted average for individuals (**unconfirmed**). Dividends are recorded gross, withheld and net, with the certificate reference. Nigerian dividend WHT is final (F12); US WHT is a potential foreign tax credit (F30). Corporate actions are explicit entries, never silent table rewrites: bonus issues as zero-cost lots; rights issues as new lots at subscription price; splits and consolidations as a quantity change with total cost unchanged; mergers and delistings as disposal/acquisition pairs at fair value. Gifts out of the portfolio are deemed made at market value (s.36(1)(a)) and need a rule (`NG-GIFT-MARKET-VALUE-2026`, draft) and a review flag (R06 m7). Realised and unrealised gains are reported separately because only disposals are taxable events.

**F6 — Forensic anomaly detection must be scaled to an individual's data (E3/E4) [S45, S46].** Useful at personal scale: exact and near-duplicate detection (same amount, counterparty, ±2 days), out-of-pattern merchants, round-amount clustering in self-declared cash items, gaps in statement sequences, transfers with no matching leg, and unexplained balance differences. Benford's-law digit tests (Nigrini & Mittermaier 1997; Hill 1995) [S45, S46]. Cleary & Thibodeau (2005) show the Type I error problem directly [S49] are designed for large heterogeneous populations and will produce false alarms on a few hundred transactions dominated by salary, rent and recurring bills; use them only on multi-year, multi-account sets and treat results as prompts, not conclusions. The product and audit roles disagree on how much to surface (see §7).

**F14a — Does NTA s.120(5) make the Nigeria–UAE agreement effective? (E2 text; E3/E4 analysis; counsel required).** Section 120(5) reads: "all extant double taxation agreements are deemed to have been made under the provisions of this section and shall apply throughout Nigeria with effect from 1st January of the year immediately following the date the agreement entered into force" (re-read 2026-11-04).
- *Reading 1 (R06's argument).* s.120(5) is a self-contained deeming provision. If the agreement is "extant", it applies from 1 January after entry into force, whatever s.120(1) says about ratification.
- *Reading 2 (ours).* s.120(5) has two conditions: the agreement must be *extant*, and it must have *entered into force*. Entry into force is bilateral. Orbitax (22 Jan 2024) reports that this agreement "will enter into force once the ratification instruments are exchanged" [S51]. No exchange has been found, and Nigeria's side of an exchange presupposes completing its domestic procedure. A UAE listing alone would not establish entry into force. And "deemed to have been made under the provisions of this section" arguably brings s.120(1)'s ratification condition with it.
- *Constitution s.12(1).* "No treaty between the Federation and any other country shall have the force of law except to the extent to which any such treaty has been enacted into law by the National Assembly" (wording per secondary reproductions found 2026-11-04 [S52], E3). Historical Nigerian practice gave treaties effect by ministerial order under the repealed CITA and PITA (E4, from professional knowledge, not re-checked). That is the most likely object of "extant".
- *One agreement or two?* Orbitax says the treaty approved by the FEC in January 2024 is the one signed on 18 January 2016 [S51]. IFC Review and Nairametrics describe the January 2024 event as approval "to sign", which would imply a new text [search results, 2026-11-04]. Unresolved.
- *Coding.* `NG-TREATY-UAE` computes "no treaty relief" (`in_force: false`; reviewed_internal; disputes recorded). `NG-TREATY-DEEMING-S120-5` is **draft**, so any computation that relies on the deeming refuses and routes to counsel (§9 item (j)). **Disagreement with R06, preserved:** R06 calls s.120(5) "an argument, however weak". We think it cannot help without evidence of entry into force; both readings go to counsel.

**Rule update process (maps onto C7).** (1) A weekly monitor of NRS, JRB, LIRS, FTA, MoF and IRS pages and two Big-Four alert feeds. (2) Any change creates a `draft` record and sets `effective_to` on the old one. (3) `sourced` once the snapshot, pinpoint anchor, second source and the seven test kinds are in place and pass. (4) `reviewed_internal` after an independent internal re-read. (5) `practitioner_confirmed` after a signed review (name, registration, date, scope). A correction creates a new version, sets the old one to `superseded`, and flags past runs for re-run. Divergent sources are stored as `disputes[]`, never resolved silently. On every rule change, every Nigerian pinpoint is re-checked by script against the stored gazette text (R06 M2). The NTAA commencement dispute (F7) is attached to every NTAA-sourced rule: for events from 26 Jun to 31 Dec 2025 the engine computes both branches or refuses.

### 4.3 Calculation audit log and explainability

Every run writes `calc_id, engine_version, rule_ids_and_versions[], rule_statuses[], watermark, refusals[], input_snapshot_hash, inputs (with provenance), steps[] (label, formula, operands, result), branches[] (one per open dispute), output, flags[], computed_at, as_of_date, yoa_mapping[]`. The explanation layer renders `steps[]` only through C2 `calc` tokens (C2.5); it never restates numbers. v0.1's "confidence is the minimum over the rules used" is replaced by the C7 watermark of the weakest rule. A run is also marked **"needs professional review"** whenever any of the following holds:
- residency is not confirmed by the user's own day-count declaration;
- the run includes any foreign-source gain or currency gain;
- it includes any treaty or foreign-tax-credit claim;
- it includes bank-deposit interest, until rule 8's dispute is closed;
- any rule used is below `practitioner_confirmed`;
- a cost basis is estimated;
- an event falls in a transitional window (2025 disposals; 26 Jun–31 Dec 2025 NTAA events);
- the total liability changes by more than 10 % between rule versions.

### 4.4 What the system must ask (statements cannot tell it)

A yearly **residency and circumstances questionnaire**, stored with timestamps and the user's attestation: days in Nigeria / UAE / elsewhere (travel log import optional); domicile claim; homes available for use in Nigeria; immediate family location; employment type and employer country; benefits in kind; employer and employee pension and NHF/NHIS contributions (from payslips); rent paid, landlord name and address (relief condition, s.30(2)(a)(vi)); whether the employer applied rent relief in PAYE (otherwise the year shows an overpayment); other assets and accounts not ingested; business or freelance activity and licences (AED 1 m and Nigerian presumptive-tax relevance); Tax ID / NIN status and prior filings; foreign taxes withheld (US 1042-S, if issued); W-8BEN on file and expiry; treaty positions taken; joint ownership shares; gifts and loans among transfers, with the market value of any asset given (s.36); US-situs holdings total (estate-tax monitor); planned change of residence (a gain is assessed in the year after disposal, F11); CRS self-certifications given to UAE banks.

### 4.5 Human-review gates and disclaimer design

Gate 1 (ingestion): unexplained differences > 0 block the period from feeding tax. Gate 2 (classification): tax-relevant categories (income, deductible, capital, exempt) require user confirmation on first occurrence of each counterparty. Gate 3 (computation): a refusal or any "needs professional review" flag disables export of a filing-ready schedule and offers a practitioner pack instead. A filing-ready schedule also requires every rule to be `practitioner_confirmed` (C7). Gate 4 (filing): the system never submits; it produces a schedule mirroring the eTax/Rev360 form fields with sources, and a checklist of documents to upload. Disclaimer text appears on every output, not in a footer: the C7 watermark of the run, then *"Computed from your data under rule versions [ids] checked on [date]. Simulated specialists, not licensed advice. Items marked ⚠ require a licensed Nigerian tax practitioner / UAE tax agent before you rely on them."* Doc 8 renders the watermark on every tax figure.

### 4.6 Regulatory-perimeter design rules

Personal MVP: single user, own data, no third-party access → outside SEC Nigeria, CMA/DFSA and CBN perimeters; NDPA applies in spirit (security, retention), formally in full once a second person's data is processed. Commercial phase triggers: (a) any security-specific buy/sell/hold output to another person → SEC Nigeria registration (robo-advisory rules) and, for UAE onshore users, CMA "financial advisory" licence; (b) connecting to bank APIs → CBN open-banking registration/licence when live; (c) > 200 data subjects in six months → NDPC registration tier and DPO; (d) cross-border hosting of Nigerian users' data → adequacy or approved transfer instrument; (e) any **paid** tax preparation for others → CITN Act s.16(1)/s.19 question and liability for pre-filled returns (NTAA s.33(4)(a), s.101); counsel before launch (F34). Design the product so research outputs are *explanations of the user's own portfolio* with ranges and no recommendations (consistent with the brief's standard 6) until counsel approves a licensed path.

The tax engine receives only the realised ₦118,039.22, flagged ⚠ under s.34(1)(b); its computation refuses while `NG-FX-OFFICIAL-RATE-2026` is draft.

### 4.9 Limitations

The framework computes positions; it cannot establish residency, domicile, "approved channels" or acceptable FX rates: these are facts and legal judgments. Rules cited are those in the gazetted texts re-read on 4 November 2026; re-gazetting or the pending suit (F22) may change them. No rule is practitioner-confirmed, and 16 of 35 are draft, so the engine as specified refuses most foreign, loss, treaty and US computations today, and every result that aggregates investment income into a year of assessment (rule 35). Several rule tests are predicate stubs until the engine exists (§4.2). UAE Corporate Tax detail for natural persons rests partly on FTA summaries rather than the Cabinet Decision text.

## 5. Alternatives considered and rejected

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Hard-coding tax rates in application code | Repeats the stale-constant anti-pattern in the brief (CBN rate in `countryProfiles.ts`); no effective dates, no provenance, silent breakage when Nigeria re-gazettes |
| Letting the LLM compute tax from a prompt | Brief lesson 4: numbers restated by a model drift; tax arithmetic must be deterministic, logged and testable; the model may only render `steps[]` |
| Editable ledger rows (spreadsheet model) | Destroys the audit trail; reversing entries cost little and keep reconstruction honest |
| Automatic submission to eTax / Rev360 / EmaraTax via scripted sessions | NTAA s.33 treats a return as duly filed only if filed by the taxpayer or an accredited agent; scripted logins also breach portal terms and expose credentials (brief lesson 5) |
| Relying on the Nigeria–UAE treaty for planning | No ratification found (s.120(1)); s.120(5) deeming needs entry into force, for which no evidence was found (F14a); Constitution s.12(1) |
| A numeric rule "confidence" score (v0.1) | No method behind it (R06 B2); replaced by the C7 status and watermark |
| Choosing one deposit-interest treatment now | The text favours A, but no practitioner has signed off; C7 requires both branches while the dispute is open |
| WORM storage for the tax vault (v0.1) | Cannot honour erasure (NDPA s.34(2); Doc 8 "delete everything"); replaced by append-only + per-class crypto-shredding with export offered first (§4.1A, C8) |
| Treating the UAE TRC as settling Nigerian residency | s.201 is a domestic, disjunctive test; a foreign certificate does not displace it absent an effective treaty tie-breaker |
| Single-currency (NGN-only) cost basis | Loses the FX component the tax law may tax separately and makes UAE/US reporting impossible |
| Full Benford-style forensic suite in the MVP | Statistically unsuitable for a few hundred regular transactions; high false-positive cost (auditor/product disagreement recorded in §7) |
| Open-banking API ingestion in the MVP | Regime not operational; licence will be required; statement upload is lawful today |

## 7. Risks, open uncertainties and disagreements

1. **Deposit-interest finality (F12), narrowed.** On the text, Position A is the default (E2): s.51(2) makes WHT final only for non-residents, s.51(3)/(5) do not cover deposits, and reg. 4(3) treats residents' WHT as an advance payment. Position B, that WHT on individuals' passive income is final as under PITA practice, has no source we could find (E4). The disagreement is preserved: some practitioners may hold B on administrative practice. Resolution: a written practitioner opinion, or a JRB/NRS circular. Until then both branches are computed in the sandbox and shown together or not at all (SANDBOX difference ₦66,000 vs nil in YOA 2026, Example 1; both refuse today under rule 35).
2. **Residency breadth (F10).** The disjunctive s.201 definition can capture diaspora Nigerians who keep a home or ties. Interpretive sources conflict on whether the 183-day limb is stand-alone. Resolution: practitioner opinion; monitor JRB guidelines (not retrievable) and early Tax Appeal Tribunal decisions.
3. **Foreign currency as a chargeable asset (F4/F13).** Potentially taxes every USD→NGN conversion at a gain; computation method, rate source and de-minimis treatment are unknown. Resolution: practitioner opinion; NRS circular watch.
4. **Text instability (F22).** Re-gazetting ordered; constitutional suit pending; KPMG catalogue of errors. Risk that rule records built on the June 2025 gazette need re-cutting. Mitigation: rules as data with `source.gazette` and `disputes[]`.
5. **Nigeria–UAE treaty status (F14, F14a).** s.120(5) deeming versus s.120(1) ratification and Constitution s.12(1); one agreement (2016) or two (2016 and 2024). A disagreement with R06 on how much s.120(5) helps is preserved in F14a. Resolution: National Assembly votes and proceedings; the UAE MoF treaty list; the NRS treaty page when restored; counsel.
6. **Individual filing deadline anchor (F17).** 31 March is LIRS practice; the statutory text we read in NTAA s.13–15 did not state it. Resolution: locate the provision (First Schedule or regulations) or treat as administrative practice.
7. **UAE natural-person registration deadline and Cabinet Decision 49/2023 text (F24).** Primary text not retrieved. Resolution: fetch the FTA natural-persons guide and FTA Decision on registration timelines.
8. **NDPC thresholds (F38) and UAE PDPL executive regulations (F39).** Secondary sources only / conflicting. Resolution: counsel; official NDPC and UAE Data Office (or successor authority) publications.
9. **Disagreement — auditor vs product on friction.** Audit view: no figure reaches the user without a reconciled period, confirmed categories and a signed questionnaire; unreconciled data is worse than none. Product view: a first-time user who must reconcile five accounts and answer 30 questions before seeing anything will leave. Proposed resolution: a two-tier display — *indicative* analytics available immediately with a visible "unaudited" watermark and no tax figures; *audited* mode unlocks tax schedules only after gates 1–3. Both positions recorded; founder to confirm.
10. **Disagreement — forensic sensitivity.** Forensic accountant wants duplicate/near-duplicate and gap checks on by default; behavioural team (Doc 3) worries that frequent anomaly prompts train users to ignore them. Resolution: default-on only for checks with demonstrated < 2 % false-flag rate on the synthetic suite; others opt-in.
11. **Estate-tax exposure (F31).** Easily ignored; no treaty protection for Nigerians or UAE residents. Mitigation: a standing monitor (class `monitor`) with a prompt to seek advice, not a structuring recommendation. It runs in presence mode while rule 21 is draft and compares the US-situs value with the threshold from the rule record only once rule 21 is sourced (C7 v0.2).
12. **Data-protection exposure of the ingestion path.** Statement uploads concentrate highly sensitive data, and behavioural inferences can be sensitive data (F40). A breach before any commercial launch would still be reportable if a second person's data is involved. Mitigation: encryption at rest, a local-first storage option, the §4.1A separation of the tax vault from behavioural data, and a DPIA before any second user (V-028; owners Doc 3 and Doc 8 with counsel).
13. **Basis period and transition (F11, F13).** Gains are assessed in the year after disposal. Which return carries them, which year's residency applies, the basis for investment income, and the treatment of 2025 disposals (CGT Act vs YOA 2026) are all open. The transition rule (33) and the investment-income basis (35) are draft, so the engine refuses those computations, including Example 1's YOA 2026 gross PIT.
14. **NTAA commencement (F7).** The gazette bracket says 26 Jun 2025; practice says 1 Jan 2026. Both are kept; events in that window compute both branches or refuse.
15. **"Taxable person" (F16).** The NTA (includes individuals) and NTAA (economic activity) definitions conflict. This decides whether a pure employee must register and file under s.13(1)(a); s.14(3) requires filing regardless.
16. **Commercial perimeter (F34).** CITN Act s.16(1)/s.19 for paid preparation, and liability for pre-filled returns (NTAA s.33(4)(a), s.101). Counsel before any external user.
17. **Rule-test weakness (§4.2).** Several tests are predicate stubs until the engine exists, and the arithmetic is checked only by this department's own script. That script reproduced v0.1's VAT error rather than catching it (R06 §7 item 9(xi)). Mitigation: VAT is now recomputed rather than hard-coded, and §9 requires an independent recomputation by the practitioner.
18. **US monitors limited to presence mode (§4.2; C7 v0.2).** Rules 21–22 are draft, so the estate-tax and W-8BEN monitors (R6) run in presence mode only: a reminder from data facts, with no threshold or validity period. Risk: a presence reminder is less specific than a threshold alert. Resolution: source rules 21–22 (snapshot and second source) to unlock threshold and date mode. Our v0.1 proposal to run a monitor on a draft threshold was rejected by the contract owner (CC-4), and we accept the reason: a wrong draft threshold could stay silent and imply safety.
