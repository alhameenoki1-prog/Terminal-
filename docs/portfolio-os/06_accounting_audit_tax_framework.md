## 1. Executive summary

**Conclusions.** (1) Nigeria's tax system for individuals changed materially on **1 January 2026** when the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (NTA) and Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025 (NTAA) commenced [S1, S2]. We read the gazetted texts directly: the new bands are 0 % on the first ₦800,000 rising to 25 % above ₦50 million (Fourth Schedule); the Consolidated Relief Allowance is gone and a rent relief of 20 % of rent capped at ₦500,000 replaces it (s.30); residents are taxed on **worldwide** income (s.12) under a residency definition so broad that a Nigerian living in the UAE who keeps a home or "substantial economic and immediate family ties" in Nigeria is *resident* (s.202); capital gains are taxed at personal rates, with an exemption only for *Nigerian*-company shares below ₦150 m proceeds **and** ₦10 m gains in 12 months (s.34); and foreign currency itself is a chargeable asset (s.34(1)(b)). (2) The Nigeria–UAE treaty is **not** usable from the Nigerian side: NTA s.120(1) requires National Assembly ratification and we found none. (3) The UAE still has no personal income tax; Corporate Tax reaches individuals only above AED 1 m business turnover, and residency follows Cabinet Decision 85/2022 (183-day, 90-day and centre-of-interests tests) [S20–S23]. (4) US dividends suffer 30 % withholding with no treaty for Nigeria or the UAE, and US-situs stock above USD 60,000 creates US estate-tax exposure [S27–S30]. (5) Nigerian law lets a taxpayer file personally or through an *accredited* tax agent only (NTAA s.33); Portfolio OS must therefore be a **preparation and explanation** engine, not a filer.

**Biggest uncertainty.** Whether withholding tax on ordinary bank-deposit interest is a final tax for resident individuals: NTAA s.51 makes it final for dividends, short-term securities and corporate bonds, but is silent on deposit interest. The difference changes the example-1 liability by ₦69,200 and must be settled by a licensed practitioner. Second: the gazetted texts were publicly disputed in December 2025 ("one law, two scripts") and a constitutional suit is pending; the versions we read may yet be re-gazetted.

**Design consequence.** Tax rules must be stored as versioned data with sources and test cases, every computation must emit an audit log, and any figure touching residency, foreign gains, foreign-currency gains or treaty claims must carry a "needs professional review" flag until a licensed reviewer signs it off.

### 3A. Accounting and audit controls for a personal ledger

**F1 — The audit assertions transfer to personal data, but the evidence hierarchy inverts (E3/E4) [S44].** ISA 315 (Revised 2019) frames risk around assertions — completeness, accuracy, cut-off, occurrence, classification, existence, valuation. For an individual, bank and broker statements are *external* evidence (strong for occurrence, existence and cut-off) whereas every category label, every "this transfer is a loan to my brother", and every cost basis typed from memory is a *user assertion* (weak). The ledger must therefore store, for every figure, which kind of evidence supports it: statement-derived, document-derived (contract note, dividend advice, payslip), user-asserted, or system-inferred. Doc 1's ingestion layer must preserve this provenance field; without it, nothing downstream can be audited.

**F2 — Append-only journal with reversing entries, never edits (E3/E4).** Double-entry bookkeeping's control value comes from the trial balance identity (Σ debits = Σ credits) and from an immutable sequence. A correction must be a new entry that reverses the wrong one and a further entry that posts the right one, each carrying `who / what / when / why` and a link to the original. Editing a posted entry in place is how reconstructed histories become unauditable (and how the stale-constant problem noted in the brief recurs in a different form). Statement-derived postings should be locked at ingestion; only categorisation, counterparties and notes remain editable, and those edits are themselves journaled.

**F3 — The reconciliation identity is the primary completeness control (E4/E5).** For every account and period: *opening balance per statement + Σ postings = closing balance per statement*. Failing this produces a named "unexplained difference" line, never a silent plug. Secondary controls: (i) statement continuity — closing balance of statement *n* equals opening balance of statement *n+1*, and page/period gaps are flagged; (ii) inter-account transfers must match in amount (after FX and fees) and date window on both sides; (iii) control totals per period (count, Σ debits, Σ credits, largest item) stored with each import so re-imports can be compared. These are engineering assumptions; the validation plan in §9 tests them on synthetic statements with planted gaps.

**F4 — Foreign-currency treatment should follow IAS 21 logic, and the tax law makes it matter (E3 + E2) [S43, S1 s.34(1)(b)].** Record each transaction at the spot rate on its date; retranslate monetary balances (cash, receivables) at the closing rate at each period end, taking the difference to an *unrealised* FX line; keep non-monetary items (securities cost) at historical rate. Separate realised from unrealised gains, and track the cost basis of each currency holding (weighted average is simplest and defensible), because NTA 2025 s.34(1)(b) lists "any currency other than Nigerian currency" among chargeable assets — a conversion of USD to NGN at a gain may itself be a chargeable gain for a Nigerian-resident individual. Which exchange rate the tax authority will accept (the Act defines an "official market rate") is **unconfirmed** and must be put to a practitioner.

**F5 — Investment accounting needs cost-basis method, corporate-action handling and dual-currency lots (E4).** Nigerian law does not, in the text we read, prescribe FIFO or weighted average for individuals' share disposals (**unconfirmed**; practitioners historically used FIFO or specific identification). The system should store lots (date, quantity, unit cost in trade currency, FX rate, fees) and compute gains under a *configurable* method with the method recorded in the audit log. Dividends: record gross, withholding and net, with the withholding certificate reference, because Nigerian dividend WHT is a final tax (F12) and US WHT is a potential foreign tax credit (F30). Bonus issues: zero-cost lots that dilute average cost; rights issues: new lots at subscription price; splits/consolidations: quantity and unit-cost adjustment with total cost unchanged; mergers and delistings: disposal/acquisition pairs at fair value — each as explicit journal entries, never silent table rewrites. Realised vs unrealised must be reported separately because only realised gains are taxable events.

**F6 — Forensic anomaly detection must be scaled to an individual's data (E3/E4) [S45, S46].** Useful at personal scale: exact and near-duplicate detection (same amount, counterparty, ±2 days), out-of-pattern merchants, round-amount clustering in self-declared cash items, gaps in statement sequences, transfers with no matching leg, and unexplained balance differences. Benford's-law digit tests (Nigrini & Mittermaier 1997; Hill 1995 — see §10 for verification status) are designed for large heterogeneous populations and will produce false alarms on a few hundred transactions dominated by salary, rent and recurring bills; use them only on multi-year, multi-account sets and treat results as prompts, not conclusions. The product and audit roles disagree on how much to surface (see §7).
